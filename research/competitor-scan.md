# Closet.zip: competitor scan

Quick scan, 2026-10-05. Closet apps checked against their own homepage, feature page and App Store listing (sources at the end). Goodreads, Discogs and Grailed are described from general knowledge of the products, not re-checked today.

## What each one leads with

### Direct: wardrobe apps

**Indyx** · "The one-stop digital wardrobe app unlocking the potential of what you already own"
1. Digitize your closet, with automatic background removal
2. Styling: paid human stylists (Lookbook from $60) or build outfits yourself
3. Share with friends; private Slack community and events
4. Wear tracking and cost per wear
5. Outfit planning, packing lists, capsules; analytics dashboard
- Free core app. Money comes from the stylist service. Pitch: wear what you already own (sustainability).

**Stylebook** · "90+ features to organize and manage your real clothes and outfits"
1. Looks: magazine-style outfit collages, Outfit Shuffle
2. Closet: searchable catalogue, custom categories
3. Packing lists
4. Calendar: what you wore and when
5. Style Stats: closet count, colour breakdown, cost per wear, measurements
6. Shopping: wishlists, test a purchase against your closet
7. Instant import: AI background removal, drag and drop
- One-time paid iOS app. Utility-first, dense, not much social.

**Whering** · "Shared closet, outfit planner"
1. Digitize: 100M+ item database, import from retailer sites, own photos with background removal, Chrome extension
2. Community: see friends' wardrobes, style submissions, moodboards
3. Analytics: cost per wear, wear rate, "intake" (what you buy), colour palette, longevity
4. Styling: "Dress Me" outfit generator, AI suggestions (paid credits)
5. Packing lists, wishlists
- Free with credits and an outfit-maker purchase. Most social and most AI of the three.

**Pattern across all three:** the closet is a means to an end. They lead with *getting dressed* (outfits, planning, packing) and *consuming less* (cost per wear, wear tracking). Adding fast (background removal, item databases) is table stakes.

### Indirect: catalogues of things people collect

**Goodreads** (books): shelves (read / reading / want to read) and custom shelves; ratings and reviews; yearly reading challenge and "year in books"; friends' activity feed; recommendations. Logging is fast because every book already exists in a shared catalogue.

**Discogs** (records): a crowd-built database of every release and pressing; your Collection (with condition, notes, custom fields) and Wantlist; estimated collection value from marketplace sales; the marketplace where anyone can sell from their collection. Identity comes from what you own: your collection page is your profile.

**Grailed** (menswear resale): marketplace first. Leads with buying and selling; "grails" as a culture; editorial (Dry Clean Only), designer pages, price history from sold listings, follow sellers, favourites. Your profile is your selling closet, not your whole wardrobe.

**Pattern:** the collection *is* the identity, and community grows from a shared catalogue (one record for "this exact thing" that many people own, want or review). Selling (Discogs, Grailed) and want lists turn a private record into a reason to come back.

## Compared with Closet.zip

### Similar
- **Photo-first catalogue** of your own clothes with optional details (all three closet apps).
- **Stats-ish views:** Closet.zip has colour shelves and brands (Overview), and colour detection; Stylebook and Whering lead with colour breakdowns.
- **Sharing with people you choose:** Closet.zip's public /@username (15) sits near Whering's "see friends' wardrobes" and Indyx's sharing.
- **Selling from your closet** (16, planned) echoes Discogs/Grailed: your owned list becomes your shop. Grailed is the obvious place people already do this.
- **"Grail" language and provenance** (where you found it, the story) is Grailed's culture and Discogs' notes field.

### Different
- **Archive, not planner.** Closet.zip leads with memory: what you own *and have owned*, where it came from, the story, style over time (TIME view, ARCHIVE). None of the three closet apps lead with history; archived/sold pieces are an afterthought there. Closest analogue is Goodreads' "read" shelf and year in books.
- **No outfits, wear tracking or cost per wear yet.** These are the headline features of all three competitors. Styling is planned (17); wear tracking isn't on the roadmap.
- **No background removal or item database.** Competitors make adding fast with both; Closet.zip asks people to remove backgrounds themselves, and friends already named filling in details as the biggest pain. Discogs/Goodreads show what a shared catalogue does for logging speed (PRODUCT.md "Later": autofill from shared entries).
- **Garment measurements, size labels, cm/in.** Deeper than any competitor; this is a reseller/collector need (Grailed listings live on measurements).
- **Gallery design, web only.** Competitors are native apps with dense, busy interfaces. Closet.zip is calm, minimal, responsive web.
- **Private by default, no discovery.** Whering and Indyx push community; Goodreads/Discogs are built on it. Closet.zip deliberately has no directory or feed (profiles are link-only, noindex). Friends asked for discovery and connecting people (PRODUCT.md "Later").
- **No business model yet.** Indyx sells stylists, Whering sells AI credits, Stylebook is a paid app, Discogs and Grailed take marketplace fees.

## Takeaways worth considering (not decisions)
- The space is crowded on "get dressed and buy less"; "a record of what you've owned and its story" is open. Leading with that is the clearest difference.
- The biggest gap against competitors is **speed of adding** (background removal, autofill), which matches the friend feedback.
- Discogs is the strongest model for where sharing and selling could go: collection = profile, want list, sell from what you own, value from sold prices.

## Audience (added 2026-10-05)
- **Whering:** founded for "millennial women who are excited to take back ownership of their fashion consumption" (founder Bianca Rangecroft, [The Industry interview](https://www.theindustry.fashion/the-interview-bianca-rangecroft-founder-of-whering/)). UK-based, sustainability and getting-dressed framing. No public age or gender breakdown found; its current Gen Z, TikTok-heavy tilt is an inference from its marketing, not a checked figure. Indyx and Stylebook pitch to the same women's-wear, outfit-planning crowd.
- **Grailed:** menswear and streetwear collectors and resellers (general knowledge). Cares about specific brands and designers, archival pieces, provenance, measurements and resale value, not outfits or cost per wear.
- **Read for Closet.zip:** what's built so far (measurements, size labels, where from, archive of past pieces, sell to friends, "grails") already fits the Grailed collector better than the Whering user. No wardrobe app serves that collector, so it's an open lane, but it's a choice to make on purpose, since PRODUCT.md describes the audience broadly.
- **Decided by Christian (2026-10-05):** the audience is higher-end collectors tracking their collection, not Whering's millennial women. They're less focused on cost. The visual direction stays quiet and collector-like rather than bold and bright. An outfit maker or randomizer is a secondary feature, not a headline one.
